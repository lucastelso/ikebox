# IPsec Client 0.8.0 (pre-alpha): sign in with your certificate

*Leia em português: [v0.8.0.pt-BR.md](https://github.com/lucastelso/ipsec-client/blob/v0.8.0/docs/releases/v0.8.0.pt-BR.md)*

The app now connects to VPNs where you sign in with a certificate from your
company (a `.p12` or `.pfx` file), in IKEv2 or in IKEv1.

## How to update

1. Install the new package over the old one:
   - Fedora: `sudo dnf install ./ipsec-client-0.8.0-1.x86_64.rpm`
   - openSUSE: `sudo zypper install --allow-unsigned-rpm ./ipsec-client-0.8.0-1.x86_64.rpm`
   - Ubuntu/Debian: `sudo apt install ./ipsec-client_0.8.0_amd64.deb`
   - AppImage: replace the old file with the new one.
2. Open the app. It offers to update the network component (**Update now**):
   nothing in it changed since 0.7.0, but it carries the version number. It
   takes a few seconds and asks for the administrator password. Coming from
   0.6.0 or older, this update is the one that brings what EAP needs.

## What changed

- **Your certificate.** In **Profiles → New**, choose **IKEv2 with your
  certificate**, or pick **Your certificate (.p12)** under "How you sign
  in" in any profile (IKEv1 in main mode works too, with username and
  password after the certificate if your VPN asks). Fill in the gateway, the
  name in its certificate and its CA, as for EAP.
- **The first time you connect**, the app asks you to choose the `.p12`
  file and type its password. It opens the file here, keeps the private key
  and the certificate in the **system keyring**, and does not keep the
  password. After that you can delete the file.
- Old files (3DES/RC2, as older Windows and firewalls export) work too.
- **30 days before your certificate expires**, the connection screen says
  so.
- If the gateway does not accept your certificate, the app says so, and
  what to check.
- Your name and e-mail from the certificate are masked in the log, like the
  username.
- From the terminal: `ipsec-client-cli secrets import-p12 <profile> <file>`,
  or `connect --client-p12 <file>` for one connection only.

## Known limits

- The key must be **RSA or Ed25519** (no ECDSA yet), as must the gateway's
  certificate.
- No smartcards or USB tokens yet.
- **Revocation is not checked** (CRL/OCSP). Details in
  [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/lucastelso/ipsec-client/blob/v0.8.0/SECURITY.md).

## Tested distributions

Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04, Debian 12 and 13, Fedora 45, openSUSE Tumbleweed and
AlmaLinux 10 (with EPEL). RHEL 9 and its rebuilds are not supported.

| Your distribution | File |
|---|---|
| Fedora, openSUSE, RHEL/Rocky/AlmaLinux 10 (with EPEL) | `ipsec-client-0.8.0-1.x86_64.rpm` |
| Ubuntu 22.04+, Debian 12+, Mint | `ipsec-client_0.8.0_amd64.deb` |
| Others | `ipsec-client_0.8.0_amd64.AppImage` |

To check the download, in the folder with the files:

```bash
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing
```

## Other known limitations

- **Pre-alpha:** validated on a real FortiGate VPN (IKEv1 aggressive + PSK +
  XAuth); the other modes, certificates included, are validated against
  test gateways only.
- **x86_64** only.
- On GNOME, the tray icon needs the AppIndicator extension.
- Closing the window closes the app, but **does not disconnect** the VPN.
